How to start a used car dealership in South Africa

The full 2026 path — registrations, premises, stock sourcing, inspections, website, DMS and marketing — written for independents who want to start right.

By Paul de Beer, Founder6 min read
Opening a used car dealership in South Africa is more than buying a few cars and putting up a sign. Get the registrations, the stock process and the digital setup right from day one, and you build something you can sell and grow. Here's the order that works.

The five steps

  1. Register your business

    Register a company with the CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission). If you'll offer finance or credit-linked deals, you also register as a credit provider under the National Credit Act — this is the compliance that separates serious dealers from sidewalk traders.

  2. Secure premises and meet municipal requirements

    You need display space and parking that actually show cars. Confirm zoning and local municipal requirements for operating a motor dealership on the property before you sign a lease.

  3. Source stock with an inspection-first process

    Buy from auctions, trade-ins and wholesale feeds — but every vehicle gets a standard condition report before it's listed. A consistent inspection process is how you avoid buying problems you can't see and how you appraise trade-ins fairly. This is where a tool like TruInspect earns its keep: guided photo capture, damage tagging and a condition score on every unit.

  4. Set up your dealer website and DMS

    Buyers research online before they walk in. A proper dealer website shows your stock with condition scores, lets buyers run a finance estimate from the listing, and puts WhatsApp one tap away. Behind the scenes you want a dealer management system (DMS) for deal jackets, F&I and your audit trail — TruDealer bundles the website, inspections and DMS in one monthly license, from R1,599/month with no setup fee.

  5. Publish, syndicate and answer fast

    Push listings to Facebook, Instagram and the marketplaces, and make sure enquiries get answered — including after hours. An AI chat on WhatsApp and your site captures the leads you'd otherwise lose at 9pm, and books test drives while you sleep.

What it actually costs to start

There's no fixed number — it depends on how much stock you carry. But the predictable costs are: company registration, premises deposit and rent, municipal fees, signage, floorplan or stock capital for 5–20 units, and dealership software. Your software should not be the expensive part: TruDealer starts at R1,599/month, TruPro at R3,599/month, with inspections included in every tier.

Pro tip: start with fewer, cleaner cars you can inspect and photograph properly rather than more cars with no reports. A dealership of 10 well-documented cars outsells a lot of 40 without reports — because buyers can see exactly what they're getting.

What trips new dealers up

  • Skipping condition reports — you end up renegotiating on every trade-in and refunding on damage you didn't document.
  • No online presence — buyers can't find you, so you compete only on price with people who walk past.
  • Slow lead response — the dealer who answers in 5 minutes wins the deal, and after-hours enquiries are usually the hottest ones.
  • Underestimating paperwork — deal jackets and F&I records need to be audit-ready from day one.
