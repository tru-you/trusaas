Cyan means the one thing to do here. One filled key per view, plus focus rings.

2

How much depth

Depth wasn't the problem — the vocabulary was. Gradient, white top lip and letterpress type is a 2012 plastic key. Thickness is different: a solid darker side, one ambient shadow, and a press that actually travels. 2c is the chosen depth and is applied through the rest of this document; 2a and 2b are kept as the record. Press them.

2a · FLAT not chosen

A shadow cast on the page, nothing more. Quietest and most durable, but on a black field the shadow is nearly invisible — so it reads as a sticker.

2b · EDGE not chosen

A 4px solid side in #226E6B — the deep cyan already in the tokens. The button has thickness rather than a coating, and pressing it travels 3px into its own edge. Reads instantly through sunlight and a glove.

2c · SCULPTED chosen · applied throughout

The edge plus a single 1px top hairline at 28% — one light source, from above. This is the most physical it can get before it starts becoming the old button again; the line to hold is one highlight, never a gradient.

Why this isn't the old button

The old one lit its face: a gradient down the front, an 80%-white lip, an embossed label. That's coating. 2c gives the object a side and states the light source once at 28% — one highlight, never a gradient, and the label stays flat. Depth belongs to the object.

The label stays flat

In all three, text-shadow and the icon drop-shadow are gone. Those were costing real contrast outdoors, and letterpress type is the single strongest date-stamp on the old set. Depth belongs to the object, not the lettering.

Secondary gets a side too

With thickness in play, secondary is a raised dark key rather than an outline — the tiers differ by colour, not by presence. Ghost is the only flat tier, which is what keeps a card of four controls from reading as four decisions.
